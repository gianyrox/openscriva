# Worldbuilding bible

A reference canon for a fictional world — the kind you keep alongside a series, a game, or a novel-in-progress so nothing contradicts anything. This template leans hard on the world model: places, timeline, objects, and world rules are all seeded, and the AI treats them as the source of truth.

## What you get

- **A five-section reference structure:** overview, geography, history & timeline, peoples & factions, systems & rules.
- **A pre-seeded world model** with one entry of each kind — a central place, a founding event on the timeline, a significant object, and a load-bearing world rule — to show the shape.
- **World rules the AI respects.** The custom instruction tells the AI to stay inside your established rules when it invents, and to flag any new fact that should become canon. Every system is required to have a cost and a limit.
- **Consistency-focused writing rules:** encyclopedic voice, internal consistency, no vague magic or tech that can do anything.
- **RAG on**, so the AI can search the whole bible when you ask it a question about your world.

## How to use it

1. Fill in the overview and your world's one core rule — the load-bearing law of its magic, tech, or politics. Everything else hangs off that.
2. Add entries through the **World** panel as you invent: each place, each historical event, each artifact, each rule. That panel is the canon the AI reads before every answer.
3. Ask the AI questions — "does this new faction contradict anything?" — and it checks against what you've established.
4. When you start writing an actual story set here, create a separate book (Novel or Short story) in the same room; it shares this world's mind.

Plot threads and narrative state are off — a bible is a reference, not a narrative. Turn them on if you decide to draft a story inside the same file.
